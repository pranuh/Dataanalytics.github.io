1. What is Data Analytics?

Data Analytics is the process of examining data to find useful information, patterns and insights that help people make better decisions.

A company has ₹100 crore in sales. Is that good?

We cannot answer immediately.

We need more information:

  • What were sales last year?
  • What was the sales target?
  • How much profit was generated?
  • Which products generated the sales?
  • Which regions generated the sales?
  • Did discounts increase?
Key idea:
Data becomes valuable when it helps us understand a business problem and make a better decision.

Business Example

Imagine an e-commerce company has the following results:

Metric Change
Revenue ↑ 30%
Orders ↑ 25%
Customers ↑ 15%
Profit ↓ 10%
Analytical question: Why did profit decrease even though revenue increased?

An analyst should not stop at reporting the numbers. The analyst should investigate the reason behind them.

2. Types of Data Analytics

1. Descriptive

Question: What happened?

Example: Sales decreased by 8%.

2. Diagnostic

Question: Why did it happen?

Example: Sales decreased because the West region declined.

3. Predictive

Question: What might happen?

Example: Sales may decline next month.

4. Prescriptive

Question: What should we do?

Example: Increase promotions for high-performing products.

Remember:

Descriptive → What happened?
Diagnostic → Why?
Predictive → What might happen?
Prescriptive → What should we do?

6. Understanding Business Problems

"Sales are low."

Is this a good analytical problem?

No. It is too vague.

We need to ask:

  • Compared with what?
  • Which period?
  • Which region?
  • Which products?
  • Which customers?
  • How much below target?
  • Why does this matter to the business?
Better business problem:

"Quarterly revenue is 15% below target. Management wants to understand which regions and product categories are responsible."

7. KPI, Metrics & Dimensions

What is a KPI?

KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator. It measures performance against an important business objective.

Examples:

  • Revenue Growth
  • Profit Margin
  • Customer Retention
  • Conversion Rate
  • Return on Equity (ROE)

What is a Metric?

A metric is a measurable value.

Examples: Sales, Profit, Orders, Customers, Quantity.

What is a Dimension?

A dimension is a way to slice or categorize data.

Examples: Region, Product, Customer, Category, Date.

Easy way to remember:

Metrics tell you WHAT.
Dimensions tell you BY WHAT.

For example:

Sales by Region
Sales by Category
Sales by Customer
Sales by Month

10. Who Uses Analytics?

The same dataset can answer different questions depending on who is asking.

Stakeholder Possible Question
CEO Are we growing?
CFO Are we profitable?
Sales Head Which regions drive sales?
Marketing Head Which customers should we target?
Operations Head Where are operational problems occurring?
Product Manager Which products perform poorly?
Key lesson: The data may be the same, but the business questions are different.
1:50 – 2:00 | 10 Minutes

11. From Business Problem to Analytical Question

Business Problem: "We want to increase profit."

Break the problem into smaller questions:

  1. What is our current profit?
  2. Which regions generate the most profit?
  3. Which categories generate the most profit?
  4. Which products have negative profit?
  5. Are discounts affecting profitability?
  6. Which customers generate high revenue but low profit?
Analyst mindset: Never immediately jump from a vague problem to a dashboard. First convert the problem into measurable questions.

🧠 Superstore Business Questions

Question 1

Which region generates the highest sales?

Dimension: Region
Metric: Sales

Question 2

Which category generates the highest profit?

Dimension: Category
Metric: Profit

Question 3

Which products are losing money?

Dimension: Product
Metric: Profit
Filter: Profit < 0

Question 4

Does discount affect profitability?

Compare Discount against Profit Margin.

Question 5

Which customers generate the most revenue?

Dimension: Customer
Metric: Sales

Question 6

Which regions have high sales but low profit?

Dimension: Region
Metrics: Sales + Profit

The Most Important Lesson

Don't learn Data Analytics as:

Excel → SQL → Power BI → Python

Learn it as:

Business Problem → Question → Data Analysis → Insight → Decision

The tools are simply how you execute each step.

Session Recap

  • Data Analytics helps businesses make better decisions.
  • Analytics can be descriptive, diagnostic, predictive or prescriptive.
  • The analytics lifecycle starts with a business problem.
  • KPIs measure important business objectives.
  • Metrics are measurable values.
  • Dimensions allow us to slice and analyze metrics.
  • Different stakeholders ask different questions.
  • A good analyst converts vague problems into measurable questions.
  • Tools such as SQL, Python and Power BI support the analytical process.
